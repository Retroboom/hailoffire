FRONT MATTER

Core Philosophy: Speak in Deeds

"Describe your deed. Then brace for consequence."

This world does not reward the cautious. It rewards the committed, and punishes the foolish. When your character acts, you describe what they do in the fiction, not what dice to roll or skills to use. The GM listens, considers the stakes, and calls for rolls when outcomes hang in the balance.

Fiction leads to rolls. Rolls lead to consequences. Consequences lead to more fiction.

That is the rhythm of play.

Time and Consequence

Time moves differently based on danger and urgency:

Rounds~6 seconds — combat, immediate peril
Turns~10 minutes — dungeons, ruins, cursed places
Watches~4 hours — wilderness travel, camp
Days/WeeksSettlement time — healing, research, plotting
  • Rounds — combat, immediate peril. Move and act once per round.
  • Turns — exploring dungeons, ruins, cursed places. Every action matters.
  • Watches — wilderness travel, making camp. Rest and recovery.
  • Days/Weeks — settlement time. Healing, research, plotting. Downtime actions available.
PART 01 // FLESH & SPIRIT

Flesh & Spirit

Six attributes. Two govern body, two govern mind, two govern spirit. All of them can break.

Attributes: The Measure of Flesh and Spirit

Your character is defined by six attributes, two each governing body, mind, and spirit. Each attribute provides a bonus or penalty calculated as:

Formula

Attribute Bonus = Attribute Score − 10

Body

  • Strength — physical power, carrying capacity, climbing, breaking, wielding heavy weapons
  • Agility — movement, dodging, stealth, finesse, landing precise blows

Mind

  • Intelligence — reasoning, research, spellcraft, logic, crafting, ancient lore
  • Perception — ranged accuracy, tracking, searching, hunting, awareness, reading situations

Spirit

  • Willpower — casting spells, resisting pain, fear, madness, corruption
  • Charisma — persuasion, deception, command, preaching, performance, intimidation

All attributes

All attributes start at 10 before modification.

Grit: The Will to Endure

Grit represents your immediate resilience, the toughness of body and spirit that keeps you standing when steel bites deep.

Formula

Grit = STR bonus + WIL bonus + Level

Character Creation

Choose one method:

Method 1 — Forge by Fire

  1. Roll 2d6. Each die result (1–6) corresponds to an attribute (STR, AGI, INT, PER, WIL, CHA). Raise those attributes by 1.
  2. Choose two attributes to swap 1 point between them.

Method 2 — Measured Allocation

  1. Assign these scores to attributes as desired: 12, 11, 10, 10, 10, 9.
  2. Roll 2d6. Lower the first result by 1, raise the second by 1.

Starting Gear

Every new character begins with:

  • One weapon of your choice from the Weapons list (Part 05), readied
  • One piece of armor of your choice, worn
  • A basic adventuring kit: rope, a torch or two, flint and steel, a bedroll, a few days of rations, and a waterskin — fill out the rest of your remaining slots with whatever the GM agrees fits your background
  • 3d6 × 10 coins in starting wealth, to spend on anything else from the Equipment lists before play begins (or hold onto)
  • If you have Wizardry, one spell already tattooed, scribed, or bound, however your Discipline provides it
  • If you have Divinity, your two Domains and your Commandments (3 Virtues, 3 Vices) written and ready

Weapons in this book are priced in slots, not coin — treat your first one as free background equipment, not a purchase. If you want to buy a second later, the GM sets a fair price relative to the armor cost scale.

PART 02 // RESOLUTION

Core Resolution

Two fistfuls of d6. One table. Five ways for the world to answer you.

When the GM calls for a roll, you and the GM each build a pool of d6 and roll them together.

Deed pool — rolled by you

Roll 1d6 for:

  • A base die, just for attempting the deed
  • Your relevant Attribute bonus, if positive (1 die per point)
  • Each rank in a relevant Discipline
  • Any other bonus this book grants the roll (1 die per point)
  • Any fictional edge the GM agrees is worth a die — good positioning, a clever plan, help from an ally

Doom pool — rolled by the GM

Roll 1d6 for:

  • A base die representing the danger of the task (usually 1)
  • Your relevant Attribute bonus, if negative (1 die per point, ignoring the sign)
  • Any penalty this book applies to the roll (1 die per point)
  • Any situational danger — outnumbered, exhausted, poor footing, a hardened target
Converting the rest of this book

Everywhere else in this book, a flat bonus or penalty to a roll converts directly into dice: each positive point adds one Deed die, each negative point adds one Doom die. A Discipline rank that "grants +1," an Injury that imposes "−2," committed Favor, Wizardry Potency — all of it. You never need to re-read those rules; just reach for the matching number of dice instead of adding the number to a total. Advantage means add 1 Deed die; Disadvantage means add 1 Doom die.

Each die that shows a 1 or a 6 is a Success. Compare the two pools:

▼▼Miss by 2+Critical Failure
▼Miss by 1Failure w/ Consequence
◇PushSuccess w/ Cost
▲Hit by 1Success
▲▲Hit by 2+Critical Success
  • Hit by 2 or more — Critical Success: your Deed Successes beat the Doom's by 2+. You succeed and gain an extra benefit.
  • Hit by 1 — Success: your Deed Successes beat the Doom's by 1. You accomplish your goal.
  • Push — Success with Cost: equal Successes on both sides (including 0–0). You succeed, but complications arise.
  • Miss by 1 — Failure with Consequence: the Doom's Successes beat yours by 1. You fail and the situation worsens.
  • Miss by 2 or more — Critical Failure: the Doom's Successes beat yours by 2+. Catastrophic failure with lasting repercussions.
Worked example

Corvin (STR +3) tries to force open a rusted portcullis. That's 1 base die + 3 for his Strength = 4 Deed dice. The GM rules it's stiff but not impossible: 1 base Doom die, +1 for the rust jamming the mechanism = 2 Doom dice. Corvin rolls 4d6: 3, 1, 6, 4 — two Successes (the 1 and the 6). The GM rolls 2d6: 5, 1 — one Success. Corvin's 2 beats the GM's 1 by exactly 1: a plain Success. The gate grinds open.

Field note

Some actions use specialized tables (Fight, Dodge, Shoot, Cast) with the same five bands but specific outcomes. The band symbols (▼▼, ▼, ◇, ▲, ▲▲) are shorthand you'll see throughout this book and on the quick reference in Part 09 — they mean exactly what they meant before, only the dice underneath them changed.

PART 03 // MASTERY

Disciplines of Mastery

Power belongs to the committed. Every advance is a choice: sharpen what you have, or claim something new.

Each time you advance, choose to either gain a new Discipline at rank 1 or increase an existing Discipline.

Weapon Mastery

"The blade is a language. You learn fluency by bleeding."

Choose one weapon type. Each rank grants:

  • +1 Deed die to attack rolls
  • +1 harm dealt
  • +1 protection when using Protect Yourself

Tricks of the Trade

"Magic bends the world, but the right tool breaks it clean."

Choose one specialty. When performing related actions, add Deed dice based on your rank:

  • Practiced (+1 die) — reliable under pressure
  • Skilled (+2 dice) — professional confidence and speed
  • Accomplished (+3 dice) — artistic mastery, recognized by peers

Physical tasks: build your Deed pool from AGI + your Tricks bonus dice.
Knowledge tests: build your Deed pool from INT + your Tricks bonus dice.

Specialties available:

  • Locks & Traps — disabling, rigging, recognizing mechanical/magical hazards
  • Sneaking & Scaling — moving unseen, tailing, climbing, breaking and entering
  • Disguise & Poison — forging documents, impersonation, crafting toxins and antidotes

Wizardry

"Ink remembers what the tongue forgets. The skin holds what the mind cannot."

Each rank grants:

  • +1 Deed die to casting rolls (WIL + Wizardry)
  • +1 Deed die to magical research and item identification (INT + Wizardry)

Spells must be tattooed, read from scrolls/books, or stored in wands/staves. To cast, one hand must be free (unless tattooed), the other holding your magical reference. See Part 07 for the full casting rules.

Divinity

"Your god may be silent. That does not mean they are not watching."

Each rank grants:

  • +1 Deed die to Providence rolls (CHA + Divinity)
  • Increases maximum Favor (CHA + Divinity total)

See Part 08 for Domains, Commandments, and the full rules of Providence.

PART 04 // GROWTH

Advancement

Every scar teaches something, if you survive long enough to learn it.

Gaining Experience

Earn 1 XP when you:

  • Miss (either tier) on any attribute check
  • Roleplay your character's traits meaningfully
  • Complete a significant goal
  • Execute a clever plan
  • Recover rare treasure or forbidden knowledge

Leveling Up

When XP equals current level × 10:

  1. Choose one attribute to raise by 1
  2. Roll 1d6 to randomly raise a second attribute by 1
  3. Gain one Discipline advancement
Attribute cap

No attribute may be raised above 18 through leveling. If the random 1d6 in step 2 lands on an attribute already at 18, raise a different attribute of your choice by 1 instead.

The pool version doesn't have the d20 version's problem of Critical Failure becoming mathematically impossible — with two pools of dice instead of one flat total, the GM's Doom pool can always roll enough Successes to beat you by 2, no matter how large your Deed pool gets. What the cap protects here is the table itself: without it, an attribute bonus can keep growing every level, and a Deed pool of 12, 15, or 20 dice becomes unwieldy to actually roll and count, while making the GM's Doom pool feel like it can never realistically keep pace unless monsters scale just as aggressively. An 18 keeps Deed pools at a size you can physically handle and keeps threat scaling sane on the GM's side of the table.

Training requirements

Advancing Disciplines requires finding masters more skilled than you. Their training won't come cheap. Maximum combined Discipline ranks = current level.

PART 05 // ARMS

Arms & Encumbrance

What you carry decides what you can do, and what you can survive.

Carrying Capacity

Your STR score equals your equipment slots. Going over that limit costs −1 AGI immediately, and then:

  • If your STR bonus is +1 or better, every additional full block of slots equal to your STR bonus costs another −1 AGI (strong characters shrug off the first few extra slots).
  • If your STR bonus is 0 or negative (STR 11 or lower), skip the block — every single slot over your limit costs its own −1 AGI.
Worked example

STR 14 (bonus +4, capacity 14 slots) carries 20 slots of gear — 6 over. The first slot over costs −1 AGI. The remaining 5 slots group into blocks of 4 (the STR bonus): one full block costs another −1 AGI, and the last leftover slot isn't enough for a second block. Total: −2 AGI.

STR 9 (bonus −1, capacity 9 slots, STR 11 or lower) carries 12 slots — 3 over. With a STR bonus of 0 or negative, there's no block to shrug off: all 3 slots cost −1 AGI apiece. Total: −3 AGI.

Weight Guidelines

  • 100 coins = 1 slot
  • 10 small vials = 1 slot
  • Light, one-handed item = 1 slot
  • Heavy or two-handed item = 2+ slots

Readied vs Packed

  • Readied — in hand or immediately accessible (scabbards, bandoliers)
  • Packed — stored in bags, requires a vulnerable action to retrieve
  • Dropping — free action
  • Stowing — vulnerable action

Armor (Stackable)

Wear up to 5 pieces. Armor cannot reduce harm below 1 unless only 1 harm was dealt.

Protection

Each equipped piece of armor reduces harm by 1, stacking with every other piece worn (to a maximum of 5, since only 5 pieces can be worn at once).

Base harm

Weapon Tags:

  • 2h — requires both hands
  • Area — affects multiple targets
  • Bludgeoning — armor cannot reduce below 2 unless only 2 dealt
  • Forceful — knockback, trip, stun effects
  • Heavy — add STR modifier to damage
  • Piercing — ignores armor on 6s
  • Quick — faster initiative and resolution
  • Ranged — distance attacks
  • Reach — outranges melee weapons
  • Reload — vulnerable action to reload
  • Rending — damages equipment, causes bleeding/dismemberment
  • Short — easier to conceal
  • Versatile — +1 damage when two-handed

Length Advantage: longer weapons have advantage until the shorter weapon lands a hit, then disadvantage until distance is restored.

PART 06 // COMBAT

When Words Fail, Blood Answers

Combat is brutal, swift, and mutual. Perfect parries are myths.

If you fight, you will bleed, the question is whether you'll make them bleed worse.

Fight (Melee Combat)

Build your Deed pool from AGI (plus Weapon Mastery and any edge); the GM builds Doom from the foe's danger. Roll and compare:

  • Hit by 2+: suffer harm, choose 3 options
  • Hit by 1: suffer harm, choose 2 options
  • Push: suffer harm, choose 1 option
  • Miss by 1: suffer harm, gain 1 XP
  • Miss by 2+: suffer harm + 1d6 extra, gain 1 XP

Combat Options:

  • Land Your Blow — deal weapon damage
  • Strike Hard and Fast — act first, deal +1 harm
  • Protect Yourself — reduce incoming harm by 1
  • Seize Advantage — trip, disarm, blind, push, etc. (GM may call for X-in-6 roll)
Why you "suffer harm" on every result

Notice every band on the Fight table says suffer harm, even Hit by 2+. That's not a typo — melee is mutual and simultaneous. Your Fight roll never decides whether you get hit; it decides how many Combat Options you get to spend, and Protect Yourself is one of those options. Roll badly and you can't reduce what's coming. Roll well and you can choose to blunt it, land your own blow, and seize an advantage, all in the same breath.

Worked example

Mireth (AGI +2) swings her Arming Sword at a Tallow Ghoul: 1 base die + 2 for her Agility = 3 Deed dice. The GM sets the ghoul's Doom at 1 base die (it's fast but stupid). Mireth rolls 2, 6, 4 — one Success. The GM rolls 3 — no Success. Mireth's 1 beats the GM's 0: a Success, two Combat Options. She takes the base 1d6 harm from the exchange no matter what she picks. She chooses Land Your Blow (her sword deals its 1d6, modified by its Versatile tag) and Protect Yourself (−1 to the harm she just took). If she'd rolled zero Successes to the GM's one instead — a Miss by 1 — she'd still take her harm in full, gain 1 XP, and get no options at all: no blow landed, no reduction, nothing but the wound.

Shoot or Throw (Ranged Attacks)

Build your Deed pool from PER (plus any edge); the GM builds Doom from distance, cover, and the target's evasion. Roll and compare:

  • Hit by 2+: hit, choose 3 options
  • Hit by 1: hit, choose 2 options
  • Push: hit, choose 1 option
  • Miss by 1: miss, gain 1 XP
  • Miss by 2+: miss, lose extra ammo, gain 1 XP

Ranged Options:

  • Called Shot — target weak points for special effects
  • Remain Hidden — avoid drawing attention if possible
  • Preserve Ammo — don't mark ammunition use

Dodge (Evading Harm)

Build your Deed pool from AGI (plus any edge); the GM builds Doom from however many foes or attacks you're evading. Roll and compare:

  • Hit by 2+: avoid harm completely, may counterattack or reposition
  • Hit by 1: avoid harm entirely
  • Push: choose 2 options
  • Miss by 1: choose 1 option, gain 1 XP
  • Miss by 2+: choose 0 options, gain 1 XP

Dodge Options:

  • Evade Harm — take no damage
  • Stay Upright — don't fall prone
  • Keep Your Grip — don't drop readied items

Harm and Healing

"Every wound is a debt. Some come due immediately. Others wait to be collected."

Taking Damage

Harm flows through these steps:

  1. Protect Yourself — reduce melee harm if chosen in combat
  2. Apply Armor — subtract 1 harm per equipped piece of armor from remaining harm
  3. Grit Absorbs — subtract from Grit first
  4. Body Breaks — apply overflow to STR or AGI (whichever is higher)

Critical Hits and Weapon Effects

Rolling 6 on harm triggers weapon special effects:

  • Piercing — ignores armor completely
  • Rending — damages armor/shields, causes dismemberment
  • Bludgeoning — knockdown, stunning effects

Injuries

"Some wounds close. Others just teach you to live differently."

Suffer a serious Injury if:

  • You take 6+ harm from a single attack (after reductions)
  • STR drops to 0 from damage

When injured:

  • Roll 1d6 on the table that matches what hurt you, adding +1 to the roll for every Injury you're already carrying (to a maximum of +3): Slash Wounds for Slash-tagged weapons, Puncture Wounds for Pierce-tagged weapons, Blunt Trauma for Bludgeon-tagged weapons, Other Harm for falls, fire, poison, and anything without a clean weapon tag, or Horror & Madness when the wound is to the mind rather than the body (fear, corruption, divine displeasure, magical trauma)
  • Suffer +1 Doom die on all rolls until healed
  • May make a WIL check (Push or better) to ignore the penalty for one turn
Reading the tables

Each table runs 1 to 7. A fresh wound is just the raw 1d6 — but every Injury already on your sheet adds +1 to the roll (capped at +3), so the wounded break faster: a character carrying two Injuries rolls the die and adds 2, making the low, survivable rows harder to land on and the top of the table (7) a real possibility. A total above 7 still reads as row 7. The +3 cap means even a character carrying four, five, or more Injuries never rolls worse than 1d6+3 — still dangerous (a minimum result of 4), but not an automatic death sentence on every wound after the third.

Lethal means the injury kills you if not stabilized within the Time Limit. "Yes, −1 [Attribute]" means it's lethal on that same clock and, once healed, leaves a permanent −1 to the named attribute — the wound closes, but it never fully comes back. Healing Time is how long the listed penalty lasts once the wound is stabilized and safely resting.

Recovery

Grit: fully restored with 10 minutes rest.

Attributes:

  • 1 Watch + ration: heal half of each attribute's damage (round down)
  • 2 Watches + ration + fire: above, plus 1d6 more, distributed as desired. Gain 1 reroll.

PART 07 // WIZARDRY

To Grasp the Flame

Magic is not learned, it is inherited. Every spell was carved long ago by something inhuman.

Magic is not learned, it is inherited. Every spell was carved long ago by something inhuman, passed skin to skin in an unbroken chain of suffering. To wield magic is to negotiate with power that does not wish to be summoned.

Casting Spells

Build your Deed pool from WIL + Wizardry + committed Potency; the GM builds Doom from the spell's inherent danger and the target's resistance. Roll and compare:

  • Hit by 2+: spell manifests with enhanced effect
  • Hit by 1: spell succeeds normally
  • Push: spell succeeds, but 1 mishap per Potency level
  • Miss by 1: spell fails, 1 mishap per Potency, gain 1 XP
  • Miss by 2+: catastrophic failure, 2 mishaps per Potency, gain 1 XP
Potency levels

Higher Potency increases effect power but multiplies mishaps. Each Potency above 1 gives intelligent targets +1 Doom die to resist.

Encumbrance Penalty: negative AGI from gear adds Doom dice to casting rolls.
Burn Willpower: spend 1 WIL to reroll every die in your Deed pool that didn't show a Success.

Ritual Casting

Perform careful, extended casting to reduce risk:

  • Declare total duration (max 90 minutes)
  • Gain +1 Deed die per 20 minutes spent (up to +4 dice at the full 90)
  • Risk: Potency 2+ adds 1 Doom die per level above 1
  • Interruption: any disturbance ruins the ritual completely

Learning Spells

From Living Teachers:

  • Must learn from someone who bears the tattoo
  • INT + Wizardry check to understand
  • Requires ritual inscription with otherworldly ink

From Dead Sources:

  • Study dead skin, scrolls, or preserved markings
  • Takes at least one week of careful examination
  • INT + Wizardry check to decipher
  • Cannot pass the spell forward to others

Tattooed vs Referenced Casting:

  • Tattooed — cast without needing to see runes
  • Referenced — must have runes visible during casting

Creating Magical Items

Scrolls — single-use spell storage:

  • Know the spell (tattooed or studied)
  • Spend 1 Turn scribing with rare ink
  • INT + Wizardry check to construct
  • WIL + Wizardry check to imbue power
  • Anyone can attempt to cast from a scroll (WIL + Wizardry, disadvantage if no Wizardry ranks)

Wands and Staves — multi-use spell storage:

  • Shape appropriate vessel for the spell's nature
  • Spend multiple Watches preparing
  • INT + Wizardry to construct, WIL + Wizardry to bind magic
  • Hold multiple charges (tracked with a Usage Die)
  • Each activation requires a WIL + Wizardry check

Identifying Magic: an INT + Wizardry check reveals properties, components, curses, and methods of use.

Alchemy: The Rational Art

Where spellcraft screams, alchemy hums. This measured discipline refines magical residue into stable, reliable effects.

Gathering Components:

  • INT check to know where to look
  • PER check to find what you seek

Crafting Process:

  • Time varies: Turns for simple tonics, Watches for complex items, days for permanent effects
  • Roll INT + Wizardry bonus if applicable
  • Modify for tool quality and component rarity

Results (the same five bands as every other roll in this book — Deed from INT + Wizardry bonus, Doom from tool quality and component rarity):

  • Hit by 2+: stable, effective item, plus one extra dose or a faster, cheaper batch
  • Hit by 1: stable, effective item
  • Push: weakened or with unexpected side effects
  • Miss by 1: ingredients wasted, no further consequence
  • Miss by 2+: ingredients wasted, and the batch turns dangerous — the alchemist suffers 1d6 harm or the GM rolls a Wizardry Mishap instead

Alchemical Effects: healing potions, poisons, bombs, stimulants, antidotes. Reliable, but requires proper ingredients and procedures.

PART 08 // FAITH

The Price of Faith

Prayer is not a request. It is a negotiation.

While wizards bind power into flesh, the faithful bind themselves to something greater. Divine magic flows not from personal mastery but from relationship, a covenant sealed in devotion, sacrifice, and moral consequence. True faith works through providence and patience, not spectacle and showmanship.

Divine Domains

Each deity governs specific spheres of influence. When creating your character, choose 2 Domains that define your god's nature and the types of divine intervention available to you:

  • War Domain — enemies hesitate at crucial moments, weapons stay sharp, courage holds when others break, righteous cause prevails against odds
  • Death Domain — peaceful endings come to the suffering, final words are heard and remembered, spirits find proper rest, the dying are granted dignity
  • Nature Domain — weather cooperates with the faithful, animals provide unexpected aid, plants offer sustenance, natural paths appear when needed
  • Knowledge Domain — understanding arrives at the right moment, forgotten memories surface, truth reveals itself through circumstances, wisdom guides decisions
  • Protection Domain — harm turns aside through fortune, safe paths appear in danger, sanctuary holds against threat, the innocent find shelter
  • Healing Domain — bodies mend as they should, pain eases naturally, strength returns to the weary, recovery comes swifter than expected

Your chosen Domains determine what types of providence you may pray for. You cannot seek divine aid outside your god's nature, a god of war cannot grant healing, a nature deity cannot raise the dead.

Prepared Prayers and Providence

Divine intervention works through preparation, faith, and patience, not magical commands.

Daily Preparation: during your daily prayer (when performing sacred acts to regain Favor), you may prepare specific prayers:

  1. Declare your Intent — state exactly what you're praying for in concrete terms
  2. Commit Favor — decide how much Favor (1–6) to commit, representing your sincerity and resolve
  3. Record the Prayer — note both intention and committed Favor on your character sheet

You may maintain multiple prepared prayers simultaneously, each with its own committed Favor.

When Providence Might Answer

When circumstances arise where your prepared prayer might be answered, build your Deed pool from CHA + Divinity + committed Favor (1 die per point committed); the GM builds Doom from how much your request asks of the natural order. Roll and compare using the core resolution table:

  • Hit by 2+ — Divine Providence: your prayer is answered completely, possibly with additional blessings
  • Hit by 1 — Answered Prayer: divine intervention manifests exactly as requested
  • Push — Partial Answer: you receive aid, but with limitations or unexpected complications
  • Miss by 1 — Silence: no divine response, committed Favor is lost, gain 1 XP
  • Miss by 2+ — Divine Displeasure: lose additional Favor or face a test of faith, gain 1 XP

Immediate Appeals

Calling upon your god without preparation shifts results down the core resolution table:

  • Spontaneous Appeal — all results shift down one band (Hit by 2+ becomes Hit by 1, Hit by 1 becomes Push, etc.)
  • Desperate Plea — if you've already made an immediate appeal this day, shift down two bands

Subtlety and Manifestation

The obviousness of the requested miracle affects the roll:

  • Subtle Providence (could appear as coincidence) — normal roll: wounds heal in hours instead of days, enemies make poor decisions, weather turns favorable
  • Noticeable Miracle (clearly supernatural) — shift down one band: pain vanishes instantly, weapons break at perfect moments, impossible meetings occur
  • Obvious Divine Intervention (undeniably miraculous) — shift down two bands: wounds close visibly, enemies experience divine visions, natural disasters halt

Commandments and Moral Weight

Divine power demands moral consistency. Your god's commandments aren't mere suggestions, they're the terms of your sacred contract.

Commandments: write 3 Virtues (gain Favor when upheld) and 3 Vices (lose Favor when violated). Make them personal, active, and thematic.

Gaining Favor: uphold your god's virtues through meaningful action, not empty ritual. The GM determines when your actions truly serve your deity's will.

Losing Favor: violate your god's nature and face immediate consequences. Minor transgressions cost 1 Favor; major violations may cost 2–3 Favor and invite divine trials.

Sacred Acts

Once per day, perform a specific devotional act to regain 1 Favor (cannot repeat the same act consecutively):

  • Meditative prayer at shrine (1 hour) — also time to prepare prayers
  • Transcribing sacred scripture (1 watch)
  • Fasting or ritual penance (1 day)
  • Tithing gold (significant amount)
  • Converting a non-believer through deed, not word
  • Completing a holy vigil or pilgrimage
The Divine Compact

Unlike wizardry's selfish accumulation of power, divinity demands ongoing relationship and trust. Your god watches, judges, and responds to your choices through the weaving of providence rather than flashy displays of power.

Every answered prayer is both gift and test, proof of faith rewarded, but also expectation of continued devotion. The faithful learn to see their god's hand in small coincidences and favorable turns, understanding that true miracles work through the natural order rather than against it.

The faithful are never truly alone, but neither are they truly free. Power comes with strings attached, and those strings reach all the way to heaven.
