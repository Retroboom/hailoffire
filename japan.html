On the plane
Fill out your Japanese arrival card on the plane — flight attendants hand them out. Fill every field including your hotel address. Have it ready before landing. Set watches and phones to Japan time when you board and try to sleep even if it's hard.
Haneda is easy
Haneda is one of the most foreigner-friendly airports in the world — English everywhere, extremely well signed, very orderly. Global Entry kiosks are clearly marked at immigration. Don't stress about navigating it.
First stops in arrivals
Two things before you exit: (1) Suica/Pasmo card machine in the arrivals hall if the app didn't work — load ¥5,000. (2) 7-Eleven ATM in arrivals — get ¥30,000–40,000 cash. Do both before leaving the terminal.
Tokyo Monorail
The monorail entrance is inside the terminal — follow "Tokyo Monorail" signs from arrivals, you don't go outside. Buy a ticket at the machine (English option available) or tap your Suica. ~45 min to Shinjuku, ¥620.
If you're exhausted — just take a taxi
Show the driver your hotel name on your phone, they handle everything. ¥6,000–8,000, zero decisions required. You don't need to figure out the subway on arrival day. Day 2 is for that.
Children's Day crowds
May 5 is a national holiday — trains will be busier than normal with domestic travelers heading home after Golden Week. Not dangerous, just noticeably fuller. Keep bags close, let the crowds flow — Japanese crowds are orderly even when dense.
Realistic timeline
Wheels down to hotel check-in is ~2 hours normally, ~2.5 hours on May 5. You land at 2:20 PM so expect to be at the hotel around 4:30–5 PM. That's perfect timing — rest before dinner, then the TMGB observation deck if you have energy.

The strategy
Push past the lower gate area — it's wall-to-wall tourists. By the second "stage" (20–30 min up), crowds thin dramatically. The upper trails are atmospheric and quiet. Go as far as feels good, turn back when ready. You don't need to summit.
What it looks like
Thousands of bright vermillion torii gates forming tunnels through dense cedar forest, winding up a mountainside. Each gate was donated by a business. Nothing like this exists anywhere else on earth.
Sunday afternoon note
May Sunday will be busy at the base. Go anyway — the upper trails are always quieter and the late afternoon light through the gates is beautiful.
Time needed
Lower loop (30 min): main gates. Mid-level (1 hr): best torii tunnels, much fewer people. Full summit (2–3 hrs): committed hikers only. Mid-level is the right call.

Practical Tips

CashJapan is cash-heavy. Always keep ¥10,000–20,000 on you. 7-Eleven ATMs reliably accept foreign cards — use them as your bank throughout the trip.
Tax refund10% consumption tax refunded to tourists for purchases over ¥5,000 at participating stores. Bring your passport to Akihabara shops — saves ¥2,000+ on art books and games.
Google TranslateCamera mode reads Japanese menus in real time — point at any text and it overlays English. Download Japanese language pack offline before departure. Use it constantly.
TakuhaibinLuggage forwarding service. Hotel front desk handles it. Drop by 10 AM, arrives next day. ~¥1,500–2,000/bag. Use on Day 5 morning (Shinjuku → Kyoto hotel) and Day 7 morning (Kyoto → KIX).
TippingDo not tip in Japan — anywhere. Not restaurants, not taxis, not hotels. It's not expected and can be confusing or awkward. This is a genuine relief after a week there.
Heat strategyMay averages 22–27°C with humidity rising. 2–5 PM is warmest. Duck into convenience stores for AC + cold drinks whenever you need to cool down. Built-in afternoon rest breaks on most days for this reason.
Shoes offRemove at the ryokan, traditional restaurants, and some temples. Slip-on shoes make this easier. Make sure socks are decent.
Train etiquetteNo phone calls on trains. Keep voices down. Don't eat on the subway. Trains are immaculately clean — help keep them that way. Queuing at marked spots on the platform is expected.
Trash cansAlmost none in public — a quirk of Japan. Carry a small bag for wrappers. Use convenience store trash cans when you buy something there.
WalkingExpect 15,000–25,000 steps/day. Comfortable shoes are essential. Blister patches available at any convenience store.
