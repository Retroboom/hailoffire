A Hail of Fire · Tour of Duty Scenario

Cordon and Search

Situation Four · The Hamlet

A US patrol sweeps a hamlet for cache, tunnels, and fighters. It has been ready for them a week.

The Idea

The enemy does not want a gunfight in the village. He wants to prepare it and bleed you: booby-trap the houses, hide a squad in the tunnels, and set the real ambush in the treeline over the open ground you must cross. The village is bait. He waits for the search to string your platoon across the paddy with its weapons down.

The Table

6'x4' (or 2'x3' Half Scale). Five or six hootches (Buildings, Hard Cover) clustered center or to one side. A road or trail through the hamlet, rice paddies around it, treelines and jungle in patches. The treelines and jungle are the Sectors, where the ambush hides. The paddy and the ground between houses is open floor you must cross.

Forces

  • US-Allied: 3-4 Units (two Rifle Platoons, a Weapons Platoon, optional M113s). Dust-Off and Escalation as normal.
  • PAVN/NLF: 3-4 Units, Total Hidden, Home Ground (booby traps, tunnels). Best mix: an ambush element in the treeline, the rest spread through the jungle and hootches.

Deployment

  1. PAVN/NLF deploys all Units Hidden: in the treeline and jungle Sectors. Seed booby traps (Home Ground); plot tunnels (default 3).
  2. US enters from one edge along the road or paddy.

Searching a Hootch

A US Infantry Team in base contact may Search a hootch, using its whole activation (no Move, no Fire). Roll 1d6:

1d6Result
1A booby trap fires on the Team (as Minefields).
2-5The hootch is Cleared. Mark it.
6Cache. The hootch is Cleared, and the find is radioed in: add +2 to the Escalation Total at once.

Clearing bleeds him. Each hootch the US Clears forces the PAVN/NLF to roll one Break Point (1d6: 1-3 = 1, 4-5 = 2, 6 = 3). Six huts is close to his whole break limit, so taking the village is nearly enough to rout the defenders on its own.

Springing the Ambush

Hidden teams reveal at least 16" from you, measured from where your team began its move (or freely if that spot was out of your line of sight). But his first reveal is his Ambush and springs at half of that: 8", or point-blank if you rushed in. That is his one surprise, and it is spent the instant anything opens up. So probe forward to trip it early, on ground he did not choose; once it is gone, grind him with fire.

Objectives

Two clocks, no turn limit.

  • US-Allied wins the moment it Clears hootches equal to its platoons + 2 (or every hootch, whichever is fewer), without breaking.
  • PAVN/NLF wins by destroying US Teams equal to twice its Unit count (3 Units = 6 Teams) and then exfiltrating at least half its starting Teams off the table (Exfiltration, Part 1). The bodies only count if the men who made them get away.
  • A force over its Break Limit loses.
Are They Still Checking Houses?

Yes, and that is the scenario. After the ambush pops, a searching Team is not shooting, standing still, in the open, and unsearched houses still count against you. House by house you decide: search and expose, or fight and lose the race. The enemy springs when you are deepest in, takes his bodies, and fades before the guns arrive.
