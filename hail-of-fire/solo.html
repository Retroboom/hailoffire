CHAPTER 01

The Briefing

Why this game, of all miniatures games, plays so well against no one.

Most solo wargame systems fail at the same wall: a robot opponent cannot bluff, cannot hide its intentions, and cannot cope with information you are not allowed to have. Hail of Fire walks straight through that wall, because its core engine already runs on fog. The rules you already own do most of the work. This booklet supplies the rest: a compact set of tables that decide what the enemy does, so you can command one side and let the war answer back.

1.1 Why It Solos

Four features of the base game, none of them written for solo play, happen to remove the four things that normally sink a bot.

Fire never asks the bot a question it cannot answer

When a Team fires, the hit becomes a Received Fire Point, not a casualty. Nobody, not even a live opponent, knows whether that fire killed, pinned, or missed until the target is forced to act. A bot does not need to conceal its plans, because the plans are already hidden by the rules. It fires; the dice decide later; the fog is automatic and free.

The enemy's tempo is already random

Order Dice mean neither player controls how many actions they get. The bot rolls the same handful of dice you do and lives with the result. No special action economy is needed to make an artificial enemy feel constrained: it already is.

The bloodiest moment needs no decision at all

When you assault into contact, the defenders respond automatically, resolve their fire points, and any that are not Ready die where they stand. That entire sequence is dice, not judgment. The bot defends a position exactly as well as the rulebook says, with nothing to author.

The game ships with a random-event valve

The Hero Die already does surprising things on its own: a 6 refreshes a hidden Hero Point pool, a 1 calls in an air attack. That single die is the natural socket for a solo oracle, so the enemy's flashes of brilliance and moments of chaos arrive through a mechanism the game already trusts.

The Upshot

The bot's real job is small. Per activation it answers only four questions: which unit acts, what order it takes, at what target or destination, and whether to spend a Hero Point. Everything else, the base rules already resolve. That is why a genuinely full-featured opponent fits on a few cards instead of a hundred-page flowchart.

1.2 What You Need

To play

  • The Hail of Fire rulebook (v.2026.06 or later).
  • Two forces and terrain, as any HoF game.
  • A handful of d6, one d10, plus two d6 of a different color for the Oracle (read as a d66).
  • RFP and Suppression markers, as normal.

This booklet adds

  • The Commander: the engine that runs the enemy force.
  • Behavior Cards: one per unit archetype, whole arsenal covered.
  • The Oracle: a d66 event table and a cut-out card deck.
  • Missions, a campaign, and difficulty dials.
1.3 The One Habit to Learn

Solo HoF asks one discipline of you that a face-to-face game does not: you must run the enemy honestly and at arm's length. When it is the Commander's turn, you stop scheming for it. You roll its Posture, walk its priorities top to bottom, and take the first action that is legal, even when a cleverer line exists. The tables are deliberately a little blunt so that you are never secretly playing both sides. Your cunning belongs to your force alone. The enemy gets dice, priorities, and the Oracle, and that turns out to be plenty.

Designer's Note

If you ever catch yourself reaching past a priority to do the "smart" thing for the enemy, stop and take the priority instead. A bot that plays a straight, doctrine-driven game is a better opponent than one you quietly pilot to beat yourself. The friction is the fun.

CHAPTER 02

The Commander

The engine that runs the enemy: one posture roll, one priority list, and a short answer at each fork.

The Commander is the artificial general who moves the side you are not playing. It is not intelligent and does not pretend to be. It is a procedure: at the top of its turn it rolls a Posture that colors every choice, then it activates units in a fixed order of priority, and at each unit it reads a Behavior Card that answers "what order, at what target." You never guess its intent. You execute its turn the way you would execute a checklist.

2.1 The Commander's Turn

When the Commander becomes the Active player, run these steps in order. Steps you know from the base game are marked; the solo additions are called out.

  1. Roll Posture solo. Roll 1d6 on the Posture Table (2.2). This sets the force's mood for the whole turn.
  2. Roll Order Dice. Exactly as the rulebook: 1d6 per deployed PL and Vehicle Unit, each 4+ is an Order Point, always +1. One die is the Hero Die.
  3. Check the Hero Die solo. On a 6, the Commander refreshes Hero Points as normal and you roll the Oracle (Chapter 04). On a 1, resolve an air attack if you are using Air Support; otherwise treat it as an Oracle roll as well. Any other result: no event.
  4. Spend Order Points. Activate units one at a time using the Activation Priority (2.3). For each activated unit, read its Behavior Card to choose orders and targets, then resolve the normal four-step activation (Declare, Rally, Resolve RFPs, Execute).
  5. Spend Hero Points if the triggers in 2.5 are met, either now or reactively during your turn.
  6. End the turn when Order Points run out or no priority can be satisfied. Unspent Order Points are lost; Hero Points carry over.
Reading Order

Posture first, because it can change which priority wins. Then work the priority list from the top every time an Order Point is available, re-checking from the top after each activation. The battlefield changes between activations, and the Commander is allowed to notice.

2.2 Posture

One d6 at the start of the Commander's turn. Posture is the single dial that makes the enemy feel like it has a plan rather than twitching unit by unit. It biases every Behavior Card and, on the swing results, overrides individual choices.

1Consolidate 2-3Hold 4-5Probe 6Press
Posture Table (1d6)
RollPostureWhat the whole force does this turn
1ConsolidateCaution wins. Units prefer Rally, cover, and short repositioning over offense. Suppressed units try to Fall Back. No unit begins an assault this turn. Fire only from good position.
2-3HoldThe default. Units act on their Behavior Card without bias. Defenders sit in cover and shoot; attackers advance only where the card's priorities call for it.
4-5ProbeLean forward. Break ties toward the more aggressive option. Advance to gain fire position or contest an objective, but only where the odds are not plainly bad.
6PressCommit. Any unit that can legally begin an assault this turn and has fire support available should. Suppressing fire is prioritized to set up those assaults. The Commander spends Hero Points freely (2.5).

Posture and role. Adjust the roll for what the Commander is doing in the scenario, so a defender does not suicidally charge and an attacker does not sit forever:

  • Commander is defending: subtract 1 from the Posture roll (minimum 1). A defender leans toward Hold and Consolidate.
  • Commander is attacking: add 1 (maximum 6). An attacker leans toward Probe and Press.
  • Commander is below half its starting units: subtract 1 more. A gutted force turns cautious.
Example

The Commander defends a village and is at full strength. You roll a 4 for Posture, then subtract 1 for defending: result 3, Hold. The garrison will shoot from its buildings and reposition, but will not sortie into the open. Next turn you roll a 6, minus 1 is 5, Probe: now a Grenadier platoon with a Ready enemy Team stranded in the open nearby will lean toward a local counter-rush, if its card's assault conditions are met.

2.3 Activation Priority

Each time the Commander has an Order Point to spend, choose which unit to activate by reading this list from the top and taking the first unit that qualifies. Recheck from the top after every activation. A unit already activated by an Order Point this turn is skipped unless a Hero Point re-activates it (2.5).

Activation Priority (top to bottom; take the first that applies)

Posture picks the direction.

Ties. When two units qualify at the same step, activate the one that is: (a) a full unit through its Leader rather than a lone Team, since one order can move or fire many models; then (b) closest to an objective; then (c) the one you, the human, would least like it to activate. That last tiebreak sounds unfair to you and it is: it keeps the bot from always taking the comfortable option. Ahead of all three, prefer the unit whose activation restores an Order Die: a PL sitting on unresolved RFPs, or a Vehicle Unit past the more-than-half line, contributes no die until dealt with, and that silence is the most expensive thing on the table.

When no unit qualifies. There is no "hold" order in this game and the Commander never invents one: every activation it buys ends in a Move or a Fire. A Move that costs more than it gains does not qualify (a Dug-In gun abandoning its foxholes, a Hidden Team revealing itself, a defender stepping out of Hard Cover into a fire lane). If no unit on the list has a worthwhile order, the remaining Order Points simply go unspent and the turn ends.

Terms, Precisely

A shot that can kill armor. Only a weapon whose AT exceeds the Armor Rating it strikes can roll a Destroyed result. A flank shot is not a second kind of opportunity; it only matters because Flank Armor is lower, so the test is always the same single comparison: AT against the rating that will actually apply to the shot.

An assault threat, and charge reach. The Commander cannot read your intentions, so the test is a board state, not a guess. The legal window is 12" (assaults must begin within it, from unsuppressed Teams), but charges are rolled on movement dice, and nobody sane gambles a fight on the far tail: 2d6 reaches 12" about 3 times in 100, while 8" lands roughly 2 in 5, and a charge that falls short strands the assaulter in the open. So the bot uses charge reach: 8" for infantry assaulters, 12" for vehicle assaulters (3d6, about the same 2-in-5 odds), measured from unsuppressed Teams. Charge reach cuts both ways: it defines the threats the Commander reacts to (step 3) and the assaults it is willing to launch itself (step 1). Behavior Cards use "assault threat" and "charge reach" in this sense.

The Leader-led spearhead. One exception stretches the band. A Platoon Leader may always move 12", and his Move Up ability grants one Team 3 extra inches ending in base contact with him, so a platoon with its unsuppressed Leader forward can push a single Team to 11" at the same 2-in-5 odds (2d6+3), or to the legal 12" limit at about 1 in 4. It is a narrow weapon: the assault wave shares one movement roll, so from that depth the rest of the platoon almost certainly falls short, leaving one Team and an exposed Leader alone on the enemy line. A human gambles on it for one prize: a defender who may not be Ready. The bot does the same, in both directions: treat the 9-11" band as inside charge reach only when the assaulting platoon has an unsuppressed Leader with the wave and the target Team is Suppressed or carrying RFPs. Otherwise the band is noise, and at 12" the bot always passes.

Recovery, and the riders that make it free. Rally is not an order, and recovery is not a separate kind of activation: whatever the Commander is doing, the declared orders are always Move or Fire. Two consequences. First, recovery riders are always on. In every Commander activation, whatever its purpose: the Leader spends his once-per-activation ability to Rally a Suppressed Team whenever he has no better use for it, moving into base contact first if needed (Leaders move up to 12" and may act after moving); Suppressed Teams without RFPs are included in the orders (a Move order, even a token one) so they take their free Rally Checks; and a Team rallied during the activation may still execute its order, so recovery and action usually share the same Order Point. One distinction matters here. A Team with RFPs that is not Suppressed is no special case: activate it when its dice are needed; it resolves on a plain 4+ and half the time comes up Ready and executes normally (just weigh the risk in poor cover, where a 1-2 kills). The RFP trap is specifically a Suppressed Team carrying RFPs: it may not Rally itself, and a Suppressed Team ignores Ready results when resolving fire points, so activating it cannot bring it back this activation; the only outcomes are Killed or still Suppressed. Include it in the orders only when the activation carries a rescue or a cheap payoff. The standard rescue: a Leader Rallies it during execution, already in base contact or moving into contact first, and on a 4+ the suppression lifts and the Team carries out its declared order that same activation. This is the rulebook's own PaK40 play: declare the Team's order as if the rescue will land, and accept that a failed Rally wastes it. The alternatives: a Move that starts or ends outside enemy LoS clears it automatically (Fall Back); or it sits in genuine Hard Cover, where Suppressed Teams re-roll their first Killed result, so resolving is nearly riskless and clears the marker, unlocking its self-Rally next activation. The Team is only truly stranded when no unsuppressed Leader can reach it: in poor cover with no rescue, leave it out of the orders and let it sit. Second, a recovery activation is nothing special: it is just a Move order pointed the right way, out of enemy LoS where possible, with the Leader Rallying whoever he can reach along the way. Step 5's Move covers it; no separate rule is needed. The order economy still gets its due through the tiebreak above: when units tie on priority, activate the one whose Move restores an Order Die.

Leader vs. Team

Whenever the Commander activates an Infantry or Gun unit, it uses the Platoon Leader to issue one order to the whole unit, or two split orders, exactly as a human would, because that is simply more efficient. Default split for an attacking platoon in contact range: Order 1 Fire with the Teams that have the cleanest shot, Order 2 Move with the rest. Default for a defender: one Fire order for the whole unit at the priority target.

2.4 Choosing the Order

Once a unit is picked, its Behavior Card (Chapter 03) gives the specifics, but every card resolves down to this shared logic. Read it top to bottom and take the first line that is legal and sensible under the current Posture.

Universal Order Logic (any unit, any card)
If…then the order is…
An enemy Team within charge reach is non-Ready and Posture allows an assaultAssault it (fire first with spare Teams, then move the rest in)
A valid, useful Fire target exists and firing does not require leaving Hard Cover while Holding/ConsolidatingFire at the priority target from the Behavior Card
The unit is SuppressedMove to recover: out of enemy LoS where possible (Fall Back), the Leader Rallying en route; never advance toward the enemy
Posture is Probe or Press and moving improves fire position or contests an objectiveMove to the best such position, staying in cover where the path allows
None of the aboveMove, direction by Posture (forward / reposition / rearward); if no Move improves anything, do not activate this unit at all

Target selection, in one rule

When a card says "fire at the priority target" and several are legal, the Commander shoots the target that scores highest on this quick tally. Count 1 point each: the target is in the open (no Hard Cover); is a Leader, gun, or vehicle rather than a rifle Team; sits on or within 4" of an objective; is already carrying RFPs or Suppressed (finish it); is an assault threat (unsuppressed, within charge reach of a Commander Team). Highest total wins; break ties by nearest. This single tally stands in for a page of target-priority charts and is stable enough that you will not agonize over it.

2.5 Hero Points

The Commander tracks Hero Points exactly as a player: rolled hidden before turn one (a 1 or 2 counts as 3), refreshed when its Hero Die rolls a 6. It spends them by rule, so you are never tempted to hoard or splurge on its behalf.

When the Commander Spends a Hero Point
TriggerSpend
You declare an assault into a Commander Team, and the Commander has a unit that could fire on the assaulters before they connect1 HP to interrupt and fire (suppress the charge before it lands)
A Commander gun or tank could get a shot whose AT beats the armor it would strike (often by flanking) but has already activated this turn1 HP to re-activate it and take the shot
Posture is Press and an assault would land this turn if one more suppressing volley goes in1 HP to re-activate a fire unit and set up the assault
A Commander unit is one activation from taking or saving an objective and is out of Order Points1 HP to activate it

Spending discipline. Outside of Press Posture, the Commander keeps at least 1 Hero Point in reserve at all times for a defensive interrupt. Under Press, it spends freely down to zero. Check the triggers in the order listed and take the first that applies; do not spend two Hero Points on the same activation unless Posture is Press.

Designer's Note

The interrupt trigger is the one that makes the enemy feel alive. The first time a defending gun crew you thought you had pinned suddenly barks back and pulps your assault a half-inch short of the hedgerow, on a Hero Point you did not know it had, the bot will have earned its keep. That uncertainty is not simulated. It is the same hidden Hero Point pool a human would hold, run by a rule instead of a grudge.

CHAPTER 03

Behavior Cards

One card per kind of unit. Find the card, read the priorities top to bottom, take the first legal line.

Each card names a unit archetype, tags whether it is built to attack, defend, or both, and lists its actions in priority order. When the Commander activates a unit, find its card, and read down the list until you reach a line that is legal right now. That is the order. Posture (2.2) still frames everything: under Hold or Consolidate, ignore any line that would leave good cover to advance; under Press, take the most aggressive legal line the card offers.

3.0 How To Read A Card

The numbered list is a strict priority order, not a menu. The Tell at the foot of each card is the quirk that gives the archetype its personality: a doctrine it clings to even when a colder calculation would say otherwise. Leave the Tells in. They are what make the enemy read as an army with habits rather than a spreadsheet.

Where a card refers to "the priority target," use the one-rule target tally from 2.4: open ground, high value, near an objective, already hurt, or an assault threat (unsuppressed, within charge reach). Where it says "assault," obey the Posture gate first: assaults happen under Probe (if odds are fair) and Press (whenever legal), and under any Posture when defending an objective that is being taken. A card's list ends where its useful actions end: if you reach the bottom with no legal line, do not activate that unit at all. Every activation the Commander buys ends in a Move or a Fire; it never spends a point to do nothing.

3.1 Infantry & Support
Rifle / Rifle-MG Both
the line

The backbone. Fights from cover, advances only as part of a supported bound.

Priority
  1. In contact with a non-Ready enemy under Probe/Press: assault it.
  2. Enemy in the open within range: Fire at the priority target.
  3. Suppressed: Rally or Fall Back out of LoS.
  4. Probe/Press and a covered path exists to a firing position or objective: Move there, ideally to a linear obstacle or Area Terrain.
Tell: will not leave Hard Cover to chase a target it can already shoot. It shoots first and moves only when the shot is worse than the ground ahead.
MG / HMG Team Defend
anchor

A fixed anchor of fire. Rarely moves; punishes anything in the open.

Priority
  1. Enemy in the open within range: Fire, favoring targets massed within 4" of each other (spill hits).
  2. An assault threat against a nearby unit (unsuppressed enemy within charge reach): Fire to suppress the charge.
  3. Suppressed: Rally in place (does not Fall Back unless overrun is imminent).
  4. No target and Posture is Press: reposition up to gain a fresh lane of fire.
Tell: holds its ground almost to the last. It would rather trade a suppression than give up a good firing lane, and it never advances ahead of the rifle line.
AT Gun (Towed) Defend
ambush

A patient killer of tanks. Deploys Dug In or hidden and waits for a shot that pays.

Priority
  1. Armor in arc where AT beats the armor the shot will strike (Flank Armor from behind the hull-front line): Fire, nearest such target first.
  2. Any armor in arc and range at all: Fire (even a bounce inflicts RFPs and uncertainty).
  3. No armor, and no longer Hidden: Fire on the highest-value soft target in arc (a Hidden gun stays silent and waits for the armor).
  4. Suppressed: Rally via its Leader if one is in contact; it does not Fall Back (it is Immobile or nearly so).
Tell: if deployed Hidden, it holds fire until an enemy vehicle enters a killing shot, even passing up soft targets, then reveals with the best penetration it can get.
Mortar Support
indirect

Light indirect fire. Reaches over cover to pin what direct fire cannot.

Priority
  1. An enemy Team it (or an observing Team) can see, out of others' LoS or in cover: Call for Fire / FFE there.
  2. Massed enemy near an objective: barrage the cluster.
  3. On-table mortar with a self-spotted target at range: direct-hit attempt (8+).
Tell: prefers to drop fire on targets that are hard to shoot by line of sight (reverse slopes, buildings, hedgerow far sides), reflecting why you brought a mortar at all.
3.2 Artillery & Observers
Field Gun / Battery Both
firepower

Heavy direct or indirect fire. The Commander's first Order Point each turn goes here when a barrage is worth calling.

Priority
  1. A worthwhile cluster (2+ enemy Teams within a 4" radius) an observer can see: Call for Fire on it.
  2. Enemy massing to assault a Commander position: pre-empt with a barrage on the forming-up point.
  3. Direct-fire gun with armor in arc and AT > AR: switch to direct Fire at the tank.
  4. Sustained barrage available and target still lucrative: continue the barrage.
Tell: hoards its limited Fire Missions for clusters and counterattacks, and will decline a "meh" target early to keep rounds for the crisis it expects later.
Forward Observer Support
the eyes

The battery's eyes. Fragile, priceless, and kept alive.

Priority
  1. Can observe a lucrative cluster for its battery: observe (Call for Fire runs on the battery's card).
  2. Under threat and LoS to a cluster elsewhere is possible from cover: Move to safer observation.
  3. Exposed with no target worth the risk: Move back into cover / out of LoS.
Tell: values its own skin. It will break observation and pull back rather than trade the FO for one more turn of fire, because a dead observer silences the whole battery.
3.3 Armor & Vehicles
Tank (Armored) Both
the fist

The mailed fist. Trades with enemy armor and shepherds infantry forward.

Priority
  1. Enemy armor in arc where its AT beats the armor it will strike (flank if it has the angle): Fire, closest killable target first.
  2. Enemy AT gun or bazooka team that threatens it: Fire to suppress it (HE ignores Concealment).
  3. Probe/Press with infantry to support: Move to a hull-down or flanking firing position, then Fire (split order).
  4. Faced by armor it cannot hurt (AR ≥ 2×AT): reposition for a flank shot or break contact.
  5. Otherwise Fire its main gun and MGs at the priority soft target.
Tell: respects anti-tank guns. It will spend a turn suppressing or flanking a known ATG rather than driving across its front, and it hunts flanks instead of trading frontal shots it will lose.
TD / SP Gun Both
glass cannon

A big gun on thin, often open-topped armor. Deadly from ambush, brittle up close.

Priority
  1. Enemy armor in arc where AT beats the armor the shot will strike: Fire from the longest range that still has the shot.
  2. Threatened by infantry AT or a barrage (open-topped is fragile): Move to break LoS / stand off.
  3. Probe/Press: reposition to a hide with a fresh armor lane, then Fire.
  4. No armor: Fire the priority soft target only if it can do so without exposing a flank.
Tell: fights at arm's length. It will not close, will not trade at short range, and pulls back the instant infantry or barrages get near its thin skin.
Recon / Armored Car Both
the scout

Fast eyes with a light gun. Extends reveal range and grabs ground, but avoids a fair fight.

Priority
  1. Can reveal Hidden enemy or Spot a concealed Team (Eyes On) that its guns will then punish: do so.
  2. An undefended or lightly held objective within a fast move: Move to seize/contest it.
  3. A soft flank target its light gun can hurt without meeting armor: Fire.
  4. Meets anything heavier than itself: Move away, using road bonus and open-ground speed.
  5. Otherwise Move to extend the screen or hold a watch position.
Tell: allergic to fair fights. It scouts, spots, and dashes for objectives, but flees armor and dug-in guns rather than trading its thin hull.
Transport Support
the taxi

A ride to the fight. Delivers troops, then gets out of the killing zone.

Priority
  1. Carrying troops that belong at a forward position/objective: Move them up (out of enemy LoS where possible), unload, and be Called to the Rear.
  2. Empty and exposed: Move off to the rear / out of LoS.
  3. Passenger-fired MG with a soft target and the transport is safe: Fire it (may only inflict RFPs).
Tell: never loiters in the open once its cargo is delivered. It treats itself as expendable but not to be thrown away, dropping troops just short of the danger zone rather than driving into it.
3.4 Specialists (Optional Rules)

These cards cover the optional-rules units. Use a card only if that optional rule is in play for the game.

Sniper Defend
the ghost

One hidden Team that reaches past the target-priority rules to hit what it likes.

Priority
  1. A Leader, FO, gun crew, or other high-value Small Team within 20" and LoS: place a Sniper Fire marker on it.
  2. No premium target: mark the most exposed enemy Team that will not give the sniper's position away cheaply.
  3. About to be revealed / closed on: Displace (remove and redeploy later) rather than die.
Tell: hunts command and support before riflemen. It would rather kill your observer than your infantry, and it slips away before it can be pinned down.
Tank Riders Attack
the swarm

Infantry that rides the armor forward and dismounts into the assault.

Priority
  1. Their tank is in contact with an enemy Team: roll Assault Checks alongside it (riders take the hits meant for the tank).
  2. Probe/Press and an enemy is within a bound after the tank moves: ride up, then dismount and assault.
  3. Enemy AT/bazooka team threatening the tank: Fire at it from atop the hull (counts Concealed, Hard Cover).
  4. Otherwise stay mounted and move with the tank.
Tell: glued to its tank. It rides until the last moment and dies with the tank if the tank brews up, trading survivability for a fast, sudden assault reach.
Flamethrower Attack
the terror

A one-shot horror at 4". Saved for the moment it wins a position outright.

Priority
  1. An enemy Team in Hard Cover / a building within 4-6" that fuel would gut (3 RFPs, ignores Concealment): close and Fire.
  2. About to be countered before it can use its one shot: Fire now at the best target in reach.
  3. Fuel unexpended and no premium target: Move under cover toward the strongpoint it was brought to burn.
  4. Fuel spent: fight on as a normal Team on its parent card.
Tell: spends its single charge on the toughest cover it can reach, a bunker, a stone house, a dug-in gun, not on troops it could suppress by ordinary fire.
Engineer / Pioneer Attack
the breach

Breachers and demolition men. Opens the way and cracks fortifications.

Priority
  1. In contact with a bunker/building/bridge the plan needs gone: attempt a demolition charge (Training Check).
  2. An obstacle, wire, or minefield blocks the Commander's axis of advance: move to it and Clear.
  3. Assaulting a vehicle or fortification (counts AT 3): join the assault.
  4. Otherwise fight as a rifle Team on the Infantry card.
Tell: makes a beeline for the hardest point on the board, the bunker or the wire, rather than fighting the open-field battle its riflemen could handle.
Company Commander

If the Commander fields a Company Commander (optional rules), run him as a floating Leader: each activation he first satisfies the highest-priority Rally or Direct Fire boost he can reach (Command Presence keeps a wavering platoon in the fight), then, if a platoon has lost its PL, uses Field Promotion to replace it. He never advances ahead of his platoons and counts as an extra destroyed unit if he flees, so the Commander guards him accordingly.

CHAPTER 04

The Oracle

The friction of war, delivered through the die the game already trusts. Roll it, or draw it.

A live opponent surprises you. The Oracle is how the Commander does the same: sudden initiative, a jammed gun, drifting smoke, a sniper's stir, a counterattack out of nowhere. It hangs off the Hero Die, so these moments arrive through a mechanism the base game already uses, not a bolt-on deck of "gotcha" cards.

4.1 When The Oracle Speaks
  • On the Commander's turn, when its Hero Die shows a 6 (it also refreshes Hero Points as normal) or a 1 (resolve an air attack first if Air Support is in play, then still roll the Oracle).
  • Optionally, on your turn, when your Hero Die shows a 1: the fog of war cuts both ways. If you would rather keep your own turns clean, ignore this and let the Oracle be the Commander's alone.

To consult it, either roll 2d6 of different colors as a d66 (first die tens, second die units: results 11 to 66), or draw one card from the deck in 4.3 and set it aside until the deck is reshuffled. Pick one method and stick with it for the game. Where an event says "your choice," you the human choose, even when that favors you: the Oracle is chaos, not malice.

CHAPTER 05

Solo Victory

Break Points still decide the day. A Clock keeps the attacker honest. A margin turns a win into a story.

Solo HoF does not need a new victory engine. Break Points, Break Limits, and objectives already end games decisively, and they run the same whether the loser is a person or the Commander. This chapter borrows the balancing spine from the Conquest pick-up scenario, adds a Clock so the attacker cannot dither forever, and defines a margin of victory so campaign play has something to remember.

5.1 Roles & The Handicap

Every solo game has an Attacker and a Defender. Role is the strongest balancing lever there is, because it self-corrects: give the harder job to the stronger force. Set roles one of two ways:

  • By scenario. The missions in Chapter 06 fix the roles for you. This is the default; just play the brief.
  • Pick-up. Build both forces with the Force Parity Procedure or Quick Compare on the Arsenal. Name the underdog (fewer platoons, or the loser of a roll-off). The underdog takes whichever role you find comfortable to play; the Commander takes the other. If you enjoy attacking, hand the Commander the defense and let it hold.
Why Role, Not Points

Simulation of unequal HoF forces showed that symmetric bidding cannot balance a genuinely lopsided pairing: a weaker force has nothing to trade. Role does the work instead. The strong force gets the demanding job, the Clock squeezes the attacker, and a light "by feel" trim of the Break Limit (5.4) cleans up whatever is left.

5.2 The Clock

The Clock is the number of turns the Attacker has to force a decision. Default is 8. It is the single dial you tighten or loosen to tune difficulty (Chapter 08).

Default Clock8 turns
Tighter (harder attack)6-7 turns
Looser (easier attack)9-10 turns

The game ends the instant either force routs (Break Points exceed Break Limit) or the Clock runs out, whichever comes first. There is no other turn limit.

Objectives. Use them exactly as the base game: the Attacker places 1, plus 1 more per two Defender units, on terrain features 6" from any edge, all starting under Defender control. Taking an objective from the enemy generates Break Points against them (two rolls). In solo, the Commander seizes and contests objectives through Activation Priority steps 1, 3, and 6.

5.4 Margin of Victory

For a one-off, a win is a win. For a campaign, or for bragging, score the margin. It also tells you whether to nudge the difficulty next game (Chapter 08).

The Commander Never Concedes

A human opponent might throw in the towel when the writing is on the wall. The Commander plays every Break Point to the last, which means solo games often turn on that final, desperate Morale Check. Let them. The tensest games of HoF you will play alone are the ones where a gutted enemy platoon refuses to break one turn longer than it had any right to.

CHAPTER 06

Missions

Five ready battles across the whole arsenal, and a frame for building your own.

Each mission below is a complete solo battle: who the Commander plays, how to set up, what wins it. Suggested forces are given in HoF's example-platoon language, so scale them to the models you own; the shapes matter more than the exact rosters. Every mission names the arsenal it wants to show off, from bare rifle platoons up to bunkers, flamethrowers, and armor.

6.0 Build Your Own

To turn any HoF setup into a solo mission, answer five questions in order, then play:

  1. Forces. Yours and the Commander's, by Force Parity or Quick Compare. Roughly balanced is fine; role and Clock absorb the rest.
  2. Roles. Who attacks, who defends. Hand the harder job to the stronger force (5.1).
  3. Clock. Default 8. Shorten to pressure the attacker, lengthen to relieve it.
  4. Ground & objectives. Terrain, then the Attacker places objectives per the base rules.
  5. Deploy. Defender picks an edge and sets up (half in reserve, may go Hidden / Dug In); Attacker deploys opposite or arrives from its edge. Then run the Commander with Chapters 02 to 04.

CHAPTER 07

The Long War

One company, carried across a chain of battles. Men are lost, promoted, replaced, and remembered.

A campaign turns solo HoF from a series of skirmishes into a story with stakes. You keep one Core Force across a chain of missions. It bleeds, it learns, it gets replacements that are never quite the men who fell. The Commander, meanwhile, escalates: each victory you win makes the next enemy harder. This layer is deliberately light, four small procedures between battles, so the campaign feeds the games rather than replacing them with bookkeeping.

7.1 Your Core Force

Pick a company (say 5 to 7 platoons plus supports) and write it down: this is your Core Force for the whole campaign. Give the units names; you will care about them by the third battle. Each mission, you take your Core Force (minus anyone still recovering) into the fight. Track three things per unit between battles: its surviving Teams, its quality (Poor, Regular, Elite), and any battle honors it has earned.

7.2 Between Battles

After each mission, run these four steps in order. They take a couple of minutes.

1 · Recovery

For each Team destroyed in the battle, roll 1d6: on a 4+ the loss was lighter than it looked; the Team returns for the next mission. On a 1-3 it is gone for one further mission (stragglers, wounded, replacements in transit), then returns as a Regular Team. A unit reduced below half never loses its last surviving Team permanently unless the whole unit was wiped out, in which case it is rebuilt at Regular quality if you spend Requisition on it (7.2, step 4).

2 · Veterancy

A unit that survived the battle and met its objective (or, if attacking, reached its assigned ground) earns 1 Veterancy mark. At 2 marks, promote the unit one step: Poor to Regular, or Regular to Elite. Elite is the ceiling. A unit that routed loses a mark, and a Regular unit that routs twice in a campaign slips to Poor (shaken). Note the mechanical weight of this: Elite troops re-roll 1s on Checks and when firing at vehicles, and shrug off more when hit; a veteran Core Force is a real, earned advantage.

3 · Battle Honors

Award one Honor to the single unit that most decided the battle (your call): the gun crew that stopped the armored thrust, the platoon that took the last bunker. An honored unit may, once per later battle, either re-roll one failed Check or ignore one Break Point roll it would cause by falling below half. Honors do not stack on one unit; spread the glory.

4 · Requisition

You earn Requisition Points by margin (5.4): 3 for a Decisive win, 2 Clear, 1 Narrow, 1 even in Defeat (higher command sends help to a hard-pressed unit). Spend them before the next battle:

Requisition Costs
BuyCost (RP)

7.3 The Enemy Escalates

The Commander does not stand still while you grow. After each mission you win, apply one Escalation to the enemy for the next battle (your choice, or roll a d6):

Escalation (d6, or choose)
d6The Commander gains…
1-2One extra support Team (an MG, an AT gun, a mortar) suited to the mission

If you lose a mission, the enemy does not escalate, and you keep your Requisition; the campaign is meant to seesaw, not spiral.

7.4 A Campaign Arc

For a ready-made narrative, run the five missions of Chapter 06 in order as a single push, carrying your Core Force through. It arcs naturally from a clean company to a battered, decorated, veteran one:

  1. Hold the Crossroads. First blood. The company learns the ground.
  2. Steel Rain. The counterattack. Your screen buys time and pays for it.
  3. The Sunken Lane. The grind. Attrition and nerve in the bocage.
  4. Breakthrough. The set-piece. Everything you have saved and learned, spent on one fortified village.
  5. Dawn at the Ford. The open field. A veteran company against an escalated enemy, for the crossing that ends the campaign.

Tally your margins across the five. Three or more wins is a campaign won; the war moves on, and so does your company, names and honors and all.

Designer's Note

The point of the persistent force is not the numbers, it is the attachment. When a gun crew you named in mission one, promoted to Elite in mission three, and decorated in mission four finally brews up in the last battle, that is the campaign doing its job. Keep the records light and the memories heavy.

CHAPTER 09

Bot Reference

The whole Commander on one page. Print it, and you will rarely open the rest.

Once the engine is familiar, this is all you need at the table. It condenses Chapters 2 to 4 into a single running sheet: roll Posture, work the priority, read the order, resolve. Print it back to back with your HoF Rules Reference Sheet.

R.1 The Commander's Turn, In Full

Every turn

  1. Posture (1d6): 1 Consolidate · 2-3 Hold · 4-5 Probe · 6 Press. Modify: −1 defending, +1 attacking, −1 if below half.
  2. Order Dice: 1d6 per PL & Vehicle Unit, 4+ = OP, always +1. One Hero Die.
  3. Hero Die: 6 → refresh HP + roll Oracle. 1 → air attack (if used) + roll Oracle.
  4. Spend OPs by Activation Priority; each unit reads its Behavior Card.
  5. Hero Points per triggers. Keep 1 in reserve unless Pressing.

Activation Priority (take the first that fits)

  1. Assault a non-Ready enemy within charge reach (Probe/Press, or defending an objective).
  2. Take a shot at armor where AT beats the armor it will strike.
  3. Fire on an assault threat (within charge reach: 8" inf / 12" veh; spearhead band per Terms).
  4. Fire on the best target (target tally below).
  5. Move, direction by Posture: forward (Probe/Press), reposition (Hold), rearward (Consolidate); Suppressed units move to recover, riders run free in every activation.
R.2 Order Logic & Target Tally

Universal order logic

  • Non-Ready enemy in contact + Posture allows → Assault.
  • Useful Fire target + no need to leave Hard Cover while Holding → Fire.
  • Suppressed → Move to recover (out of LoS; Leader Rallies en route), never advance.
  • Probe/Press + moving helps → Move (in cover).
  • Else → Move by Posture (forward / reposition / rearward), or do not activate.

Target tally (+1 each, highest wins, ties = nearest)

  • Target in the open (no Hard Cover).
  • Leader, gun, or vehicle (not a rifle Team).
  • On or within 4" of an objective.
  • Already carrying RFPs or Suppressed.
  • Assault threat: unsuppressed, within charge reach (8" inf / 12" veh).
R.3 Hero Points & Oracle

Commander spends a Hero Point to…

  • Interrupt and fire on your assault before it lands.
  • Re-activate a gun/tank for a shot where AT beats the armor struck.
  • Re-activate a fire unit to complete an assault set-up (Press).
  • Activate a unit that can take or save an objective.

Oracle fires when…

  • Commander Hero Die shows 6 or 1.
  • (Optional) your Hero Die shows 1.
  • Roll d66 or draw a card; resolve; "your choice" means you pick.
The Golden Rule

Run the enemy straight. Roll its Posture, read the priorities top to bottom, take the first legal line, even when a cleverer play exists. Your cunning is for your own force. The Commander gets dice, priorities, and the Oracle, and that is a fight.

CHAPTER 10

Worked Example

The Commander runs a full turn: Posture, priority, a target tally, an Oracle event, and a Hero Point that ruins your afternoon.

Rules make sense once you watch them move. Here is a single solo turn played in full. You command a US rifle company attacking a village; the Commander holds it with a German grenadier platoon, an HMG, and a hidden PaK40. We open mid-battle, on the Commander's turn, so you can see every solo subsystem fire at once. Read it against Chapters 2 to 4.

10.1 The Situation
  • The Commander (Defender) holds a walled village. A Grenadier Platoon (PL + 4 Rifle/MG Teams, one carrying 1 RFP) is in the buildings. An HMG Team covers the open approach. A PaK40, still Hidden, watches the road.
  • You (Attacker) have two Rifle Platoons at the edge of a wheatfield 7" out, a Sherman platoon on the road, and one objective already yours; two more sit in the village, Commander-held.
  • It is turn 4 of an 8-turn Clock. The Commander is at full strength. Hero Points: yours 3, the Commander's hidden.
10.2 Step 1 · Posture

You roll 1d6 for the Commander's Posture: 5. It is defending, so subtract 1: result 4, Probe. Not a sit-still turn. The garrison will lean forward where the odds are fair, and will take an assault on any of your Teams caught non-Ready in reach.

10.3 Step 2 · Order Dice & the Hero Die

The Commander has two order-generating units contributing dice (the Grenadier PL and the HMG; the hidden PaK40's AT platoon PL also rolls). Three dice, one of them the Hero Die. Rolls: 4, 2, and the Hero Die 6.

Two 4+ successes, plus the automatic +1: 3 Order Points. And the Hero Die is a 6: the Commander refreshes its Hero Points (discard the hidden pool, roll 1d6 kept secret: it comes up 4, so it now hides 4 HP), and the Oracle speaks.

The Oracle

You roll 2d6 of different colors as a d66: the dark die reads 5, the light die reads 2, so 52, Ranging Shots. "The Commander's next barrage this game skips its deviation roll." The Commander has no artillery on this table, so the event simply banks: a note that the first time it ever calls fire, it lands true. Harmless now, ominous later. You move on.

10.4 Step 3 · Spend the Order Points

Order Point 1 · walk the priority

You read the Activation Priority from the top. Step 1 (assault a non-Ready enemy): none of your Teams are in contact, so no. Step 2 (a penetrating shot at armor): the hidden PaK40 has your Sherman platoon on the road, 16" off, squarely in arc. AT 5 versus front Armor 2 is a clean penetrating shot. Step 2 fires.

The Commander activates the PaK40, which reveals from Hidden as it fires. Its card confirms the choice: priority 1, armor where AT > AR. It targets the nearest Sherman. Declare Fire, resolve its own RFPs (none), execute: RoF 2 at 4+ rolls 5, 6, 2 hits. First hit to the target Sherman, second to another within 4". Penetration on the first: AT 5 vs AR 2, roll the difference (3 dice) hunting 6s: 2, 4, 6. One Sherman destroyed. The second Sherman takes its penetration roll: 1, 3, 4, no 6, so RFPs equal to the gun's Firepower instead: 1 RFP, and nobody yet knows if that crew is dead or just rattled. The fog is automatic; you did not have to decide a thing for the enemy.

Order Point 2 · recheck from the top

The board changed, so you recheck. Step 1: still no non-Ready enemy in contact. Step 2: the PaK40 already fired (it would need a Hero Point to fire again). Step 3 (fire on an assault threat): your leading Rifle Platoon is unsuppressed and sits 7" from the village wall, inside infantry charge reach (8"). That is the whole test; no guessing about your plans required. Had it been sitting at 12", the Commander would have shrugged: even a Leader-led spearhead lands from there barely one time in four, with the rest of the wave stranded behind it. Step 3 fires.

The Commander activates the Grenadier Platoon through its PL. Now the target tally picks the aim point. Your two forward Rifle/MG Teams are both candidates, and both are assault threats (each within charge reach of the wall), so that point cancels out. Team A stands at the wheatfield's open edge (Concealment only): +1 for the open. Team B is behind a low wall: no bonus. Team A tallies higher, so the garrison concentrates on Team A. Under Probe, and per the Grenadier card, the PL splits: Order 1 Fire (three Teams at Team A), Order 2 held. Fire resolves: the volume lands 2 hits on Team A, which had no cover to speak of. It now carries 2 RFPs. Whether that is a pin or a grave, you learn only when Team A next acts.

Order Point 3 · the Probe leans forward

Recheck. Step 1: no. Step 2: no. Step 3 again: Team B is still an unsuppressed Team inside charge reach of the wall, so the threat step fires twice this turn. The HMG has it in its lane, and its card agrees (an assault threat against a nearby unit). The HMG fires: RoF 4 needing 5+ (Team B is Concealed by the wall), rolls 3, 5, 5, 6, 3 hits, but no non-vehicle Team takes more than one hit per Fire order and Team B is the lone target in a lane, so 1 lands: Team B, 1 RFP. Two Order Points of fire have salted your whole assault line with uncertainty.

10.5 Your Turn, and the Hero Point

Order Points spent, the Commander ends its turn. You become Active, resolve your Team A's RFPs as it activates (2 RFPs, open ground: it dies, the wheatfield claims it), and commit the rest of the platoon to the assault anyway, because the objective will not take itself. Your PL declares an assault into the nearest Grenadier Team, and your Teams roll into contact.

Interrupt

The moment your assault is declared into contact, the Commander's Hero Point trigger fires: "you declare an assault into a Commander Team, and the Commander has a unit that could fire on the assaulters first." It spends 1 of its hidden 4 HP to interrupt and activates the HMG, which pours fire into your charging Teams before they connect. Two more RFPs land across the assaulters. When they crash in and resolve those fresh RFPs, one of your Teams is Suppressed, not Ready, and dies on the wall without striking a blow. The garrison you thought you had pinned had a reserve of nerve you could not see, run by a rule, not a hunch. The assault stalls one Team short. Turn 4 ends with the Clock ticking and the village still German.

10.6 What Just Happened

In one turn the Commander used every solo subsystem, and you never once guessed its intent:

  • Posture set the mood (Probe): forward, but not reckless.
  • Activation Priority chose its actions in a fixed order: the tank-killing shot first, then the assault threats inside charge reach, one after the other.
  • The Behavior Cards confirmed each unit's job and its Tells (the PaK40's ambush, the HMG's lane).
  • The target tally picked Team A over Team B without a chart or an argument.
  • The Oracle banked a future menace (Ranging Shots).
  • The Hero Point interrupt delivered the surprise a live opponent would, from a hidden pool you had no way to read.

None of it required you to scheme for the enemy. You rolled, you read the tables, you took the first legal line, and the war answered back. That is Lone Commander: the whole company-level fight of Hail of Fire, fog and fury intact, fought alone.
